## What make.fun is (in plain words)

[make.fun](https://make.fun) is a bounty board for software. Someone posts a challenge
with a cash prize — build a small game, write a guide, fix a bug, ship a tiny app —
and anyone can enter. Your AI coding agent does most of the building; you direct it,
check the result, and submit. Winners are paid in USDC on Solana.

Three things make it different from a normal freelance board:

1. **You do not need a client.** The bounty is the client. The rules, the deadline,
   and the prize are written down before you start.
2. **Your agent is your teammate.** You describe what to build, the agent writes the
   code, and you review it. People who have never shipped software before can enter.
3. **You can earn from referrals too.** When someone signs up through your link and
   becomes a real, active user, you earn a referral reward on top of any prize money.
   That is what this guide's link is for — and what the bring-the-humans-02 bounty
   in the badges above is measuring.

You do not pay to enter. Entering a bounty is free. The only things you spend are
time and attention, which is exactly why a weekly routine matters: it protects both.

## Your first week at a glance

If you are starting from zero, your first week looks slightly different from a
normal week, because half of it is setup. Here is the shape:

- **Day 1 (today):** finish the 15-minute setup above. Read the rest of this guide

- **Monday:** scan the board and pick your first bounty. Prefer a small one with a
  clear deliverable and a deadline at least a week away.
- **Tuesday:** write a one-page plan (the Tuesday section shows you how).
- **Wednesday–Friday:** build in daily blocks. Aim for a rough working version by
  Thursday night, not a perfect one.

  beats a perfect entry that never leaves your laptop.
- **Sunday:** check what you earned or learned, share your referral link once,

After week one, the routine repeats. The second week is easier than the first
because setup is done; the third week is easier than the second because you have a
submitted entry to learn from.

## Monday — Scan the board, pick ONE bounty

Monday's job is a decision, not a build. Give it a full hour and stop when the hour
ends.

### How to scan (30 minutes)

1. Open the board and sort by deadline — soonest first.
2. Ignore anything closing in under 48 hours. You cannot do good work in a panic
   on your first week, and a rushed entry teaches you the wrong lessons.
3. Read the requirements of anything that catches your eye. Skip anything whose
   rules you do not understand after one careful read; confusion now becomes a
   rejected entry later.
4. Write down 3 candidates: the bounty name, the prize, the deadline, and one
   sentence on what it asks for.

### How to pick (30 minutes)

Score each candidate out of 10 on three questions:

- **Can I build this?** Do I (or my agent) have the skills, or can I learn them
  inside the week? Be honest. A 6 here is fine; a 3 is a no.
- **Is the prize worth the week?** Divide the prize by the hours you will spend.
  A $100 prize for 7 hours is about $14 an hour if you win — and you will not win
  every time, so treat the prize as a bonus, not a wage. The real pay is the skill
  and the referrals you build along the way.
- **Is the deadline real for me?** Does it leave room for one bad day? If a bounty
  closes on Friday and you can only build Wednesday–Friday, you have no margin.
  Pick the one with slack.

Pick the highest total. Then — this is the important part — **write the other two
down and forget them until next Monday.** Chasing two bounties in one week is how
beginners finish neither. One bounty, one week, one submit.

> **Monday's output:** one bounty name, its deadline, and its rules link, written
> at the top of your weekly tracker (see below). If you cannot write it down, you
> have not picked yet.

## Tuesday — Plan and score the bounty before you build

Tuesday is the cheapest day to make mistakes, because mistakes on paper cost
nothing. Do not write production code on Tuesday. Plan.

### Step 1 — Read the rules twice

Read the bounty page once for the story (what is wanted) and once for the
checklist (what exactly must be delivered, in what format, by when). Write the
checklist down in your own words. Pay attention to:

- The exact deliverable: a repository? a video? a deployed link? a file with an
  exact name?
- Size and format limits (file size caps, image dimensions, video length).
- Anything that disqualifies an entry — wrong file name, missing license, private
  repository, late submission. These are free points; losing them is just
  carelessness.

### Step 2 — Turn the judging criteria into a scorecard

If the bounty says how it is judged, turn that into a small table. If it does not
say, invent a sensible one: correctness (does it do what was asked?), completeness
(is every deliverable present?), quality (would a stranger find it good?), and
packaging (is it submitted correctly?). Give each a weight that adds up to 100 and
score your plan against it honestly. The criterion with the highest weight and the
lowest score is where your build time goes first.

Example scorecard for a "build a small game" bounty:

| Criterion | Weight | My plan's score (1–10) | What would raise it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| It plays correctly | 40 | 7 | Test every level myself |
| It matches the theme | 30 | 8 | One clear theme, not three |
| It is fun for 2 minutes | 20 | 5 | Add one surprising moment |
| Submitted correctly | 10 | 9 | Checklist before submitting |

The weakest high-weight row — "fun for 2 minutes" here — is Wednesday's first job.

### Step 3 — Sketch the smallest version that could win

Write down the minimum version of the entry: the smallest thing that satisfies
every item on the checklist. That is your Wednesday target. Everything beyond it
is polish, and polish only happens after the minimum exists. Most failed entries
are not too small; they are half of something too big.

### Step 4 — Set up the workspace

Create the repository (public, if the bounty needs one), add the license the
bounty asks for, and make a first empty commit. Test that you can push. Finding
out on Saturday that you cannot push is a miserable way to lose a week.

> **Tuesday's output:** a written checklist, a scorecard, and a one-paragraph sketch
> of the minimum version, plus a repository you can push to. If Tuesday ends and
> you have code but no plan, you did Monday's job twice and skipped Tuesday's.

## Wednesday to Friday — Build in small daily blocks

This is the bulk of the week, and the rule for all three days is the same: **small
blocks, visible progress, stop on time.**

### Wednesday — The minimum version

Build only the minimum version you sketched on Tuesday. No extra features, no
polish, no redesigns. By the end of Wednesday you should have something that, if
the deadline were tonight, you could submit without shame. It will be rough. That
is fine — rough and submittable beats polished and nonexistent.

Work in this loop, and repeat it until the block ends:

1. Tell your agent one small task ("add the main screen", "write the first
   section of the guide").
2. Read what it produced. Run it if it is code; read it aloud if it is writing.
3. Fix what is wrong before asking for the next thing. Never stack three
   unreviewed changes — that is how you end up with a bug you cannot find.

### Thursday — The weakest high-weight criterion

Look at Tuesday's scorecard. Work on the weakest row with the highest weight.
That is the single highest-value thing you can do all week. For most people this
is the day the entry goes from "technically complete" to "actually good".

Stop adding features at lunchtime on Thursday. After that, only fix and improve
what exists. New features after Thursday are how entries break on Saturday.

### Friday — Polish and the pre-submit check

Friday is polish day: fix the rough edges, tighten the writing, test the whole
thing end to end as a stranger would. Then run the pre-submit check:

- [ ] Every deliverable from Tuesday's checklist exists, with the exact name
      and format the rules ask for.
- [ ] The entry opens/runs/reads correctly from a clean start — not just on
      your machine with your files open.
- [ ] The referral code or link is present where the bounty requires it (for a
      guide bounty, in the README — for this one, that is code `n14zczqk`).
- [ ] The repository is public and the license file is present.
- [ ] Nothing secret is in the repository: no API keys, no private keys, no
      passwords, no personal data. Search for them before you submit, not after.

If any box is unchecked, fix it on Friday. Saturday is for submitting, not for
discovering that your entry is broken.

> **Daily rule:** when the block ends, stop. Write one line in your tracker about
> what you finished and what is next. Future-you on Saturday will be grateful.

## Saturday — Submit and review

Saturday has two jobs: submit, then review honestly while the week is still fresh.

### Submit before noon if you can

Submit early in the day. Late-day submissions collide with tiredness, slow
internet, and every other entrant submitting at once. Follow the bounty page's
submit steps exactly, and read the confirmation or receipt it gives you. If it
reports a problem — a missing file, a failed check — fix that problem now, while
you still have Saturday left. Do not assume a submission went through; verify it.

### The Saturday review (30 minutes)

Answer these five questions in writing, in your tracker:

1. What did I finish that I am proud of?
2. What broke, and why? (One honest sentence. "I started too big" counts.)
3. What would I do differently next week?
4. Did I stay inside my time blocks, or did the week eat my evenings?
5. What is one thing I learned that I can reuse?

This review is the compound interest of the routine. Five honest lines a week,
and after a month you have a personal playbook no guide can give you — including
this one.

## Sunday — Claim, refer, and plan next week

Sunday is light: 30 minutes, no building.

### Claim what you earned

Check your rewards and referral earnings on your make.fun profile. If you placed
in a bounty, follow the claim steps on the bounty page and note the deadline for
claiming — prizes that are not claimed in time can pass to the next entry, so do
not let a win expire in your inbox. If you did not place, note that too, without
drama: most weeks do not pay, and the routine is what keeps you in the game until
one does.

### Grow referrals — once, honestly

Referrals are the slow, compounding half of make.fun income. **Your** link is your
own, from the Referrals tab of your profile — mine is
[https://make.fun/r/n14zczqk](https://make.fun/r/n14zczqk) (code `n14zczqk`), and
yours will look the same with your code in place of mine. Share it where it
genuinely helps someone: a friend who would like bounties, a post that actually
teaches something (like this guide), a community where you are a real member. One honest share a week beats ten spammy ones. Never buy signups, never
fake accounts, never share an IP cluster's worth of signups — make.fun's referral
check verifies real humans, and fake ones never count and can get entries
disqualified. The section below explains exactly how that check works.

### Plan next week (10 minutes)

Glance at the board. Note one or two bounties that open or close next week. Do not
pick yet — that is Monday's job. Just make sure Monday starts with a shortlist
instead of a blank page.

Then close the laptop. The routine works because Sunday ends it. Rest is part of
the schedule, not a reward for finishing it.

## The weekly tracker

Copy this table into a note, a spreadsheet, or the top of a scratch file, and fill
it in every day. It takes two minutes a day and it is the difference between "I
think I had a good week" and "I know what happened".

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bounty** | | | | | | | |
| **Done today** | | | | | | | |
| **Minutes spent** | | | | | | | |
| **Blocked on** | | | | | | | |
| **Tomorrow's first task** | | | | | | | |

At the end of the week, add three lines underneath:

- **Submitted?** Yes / No — and if no, why, in one sentence.
- **Earned this week:** $___ in prizes, $___ in referral rewards.
- **One lesson for next week:** ___

Keep every week's tracker. After four weeks, read them together: you will see
your real pace, your real failure modes, and the exact week you got faster. That
is data no motivation post can replace. If it helps, keep a simple streak count at
the top of each week's tracker — "weeks in a row I submitted" — and protect that
number the way a runner protects a run streak. A 4-week streak of small submitted
entries is worth more than one heroic week followed by a month of nothing.

## When the week goes wrong

A routine that only works on perfect weeks is not a routine; it is a wish. Here
is what to do when each common failure hits.

### You missed a day (or two)

Do not try to "catch up" by doubling the next day. Doubling is how you burn out
and miss the day after that too. Instead, shrink: do a 25-minute version of the
missed block the next morning, and let the rest go. The week survives a missed day;
it does not survive a heroic catch-up that wrecks the next three.

### The bounty turns out too hard

You discover on Wednesday that the build is twice as big as Tuesday's sketch. You
have two honest options: (a) cut the scope in half and submit the smaller version
— a complete small entry beats an incomplete big one; or (b) abandon this bounty
and spend the rest of the week on referrals and learning, then pick better on
Monday. What you must not do is silently keep building the impossible version
until Saturday, then submit nothing. Decide by Thursday noon at the latest.

### You are burned out

If opening the project feels physically heavy, stop building for the day and do
the lightest useful thing instead: read a winning entry, tidy your tracker, or
share your referral link with a real note about what you learned. One light day
protects the routine; pushing through burnout kills the next two weeks. If burnout
lasts more than a week, take a full week off from bounties and keep only the Sunday
30 minutes. The board will still be there.

### Your entry was rejected or scored badly

Read the rejection or result carefully — it usually names the exact problem (a
missing file, a wrong format, a failed check). Fix that one thing and write it in
your tracker under "never again". A rejection with a named cause is a free lesson;
a rejection you ignore is a subscription to repeating it. Most rejections are
checklist failures, not quality failures, which means they are the easiest kind to
stop having.

### Life happened

A routine is a default, not a contract. If a week is genuinely impossible, mark
it "life week" in your tracker and move on without guilt. The test of a routine is
not whether you never miss a week; it is whether you come back the Monday after.

## Common mistakes that cost beginners money and time

1. **Entering three bounties at once.** You finish none of them well. One bounty
   per week.
2. **Building before planning.** Tuesday exists for a reason. An hour of planning
   saves a day of rebuilding.
3. **Adding features on Friday.** Friday is polish and checking. New features on
   Friday break on Saturday.
4. **Submitting at the last minute.** Submit Saturday morning. Deadlines do not
   move for slow uploads.
5. **Copying another entry.** Entries are checked against each other; a copy is
   refused, and it teaches you nothing. Write your own guide, build your own
   entry.
6. **Putting secrets in a repository.** API keys, wallet keypairs, and passwords
   never belong in a public repo, in any commit, in any encoding. Once pushed,
   they are public forever.
7. **Treating referrals as a trick.** Fake signups do not count and can
   disqualify you. Real humans, real shares, real patience.
8. **Skipping the Saturday review.** Without it, you repeat the same week four
   times and wonder why nothing improves.
9. **Quitting after one bad week.** Almost nobody wins their first bounty. The
   people on the leaderboard are the ones who kept the routine through the
   losing weeks.
10. **Waiting for a perfect week to start.** There is no perfect week. Start this
    Monday, with the week you actually have.

## FAQ

**Do I need to know how to code?**

No. Your AI agent writes the code; you direct it, test it, and make the
decisions. Knowing a little helps you review better, but plenty of entrants start
with no coding background and learn by reviewing what their agent builds.

**How much money can I really make?**

Honestly: most weeks, nothing. Bounties pay the winners, and there are many
entrants. Treat prizes as a bonus and referrals as the slow compounding income.
Anyone promising guaranteed money from make.fun is selling you something.

**How much time does the routine take?**

About 7 hours a week at the full pace, about 4 at the minimum pace. If you have
less than 4 hours, do Monday, Saturday, and Sunday only, and pick the smallest
bounties. A small routine you keep beats a big one you quit.

**What if I miss the deadline for a bounty I picked?**

You learn from it and pick again on Monday. Do not submit late — late entries do
not count. Note the miss in your tracker and check next time whether the deadline
was unrealistic or your week was.

**Can I do this with friends?**

Yes — a team enters under one account and splits any prize itself. One account
per person is the rule, so do not create extra accounts for teammates.

**Is make.fun legit?**

make.fun is a real bounty platform that pays prizes in USDC on Solana. It is not
makefun.ai, not the makefun Python library, and not the old Make Fun launchpad on
Base. As with any platform, read the rules of each bounty before you enter, and
never pay anyone to enter — entering is free.
